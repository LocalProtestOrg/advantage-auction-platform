Lesson 1

Organize your auction location

A sale laid out once, in lot order, is faster to photograph, faster to catalog and much faster on pickup day.

Set up zones

  • Work tables in one room or garage with good light and a walkway around each table.
  • A photo station: one spot with a plain backdrop and steady light, close to the tables.
  • Named staging zones (Table A, Garage Shelf 2, Dining Room). Write the zone for every lot on the inventory worksheet.
  • Not for sale: close rooms and clearly label or remove anything that is not in the auction.
  • Safety: anchor or lay down tall, top-heavy furniture while it is staged.

Mark tables and lots with painter's tape

  • Blue painter's tape comes off wood, glass and most finishes cleanly. Never tape gilding, paper, flaking paint, upholstery or textiles; use a tie-on tag or a label on the table instead.
  • Write the zone on the corner of each table and put a lot label on or beside each lot.
  • Large furniture gets its label on the back or underside, plus one on the floor in front of it.

Number lots so pickup is easy

  • Number in catalog order; lots close in that order.
  • Keep zones in runs: 101 to 140 for the kitchen, 141 to 180 for the garage.
  • Never reuse a number. If a lot is withdrawn, leave its number unused.
  • Match three places: the label, the worksheet and the catalog.
Pro Tip

Keep tape, a marker, label sheets and the inventory worksheet on one clipboard. Write each lot down the moment it gets a number, with its zone.

Lesson 2

Build lots that encourage competitive bidding

Prices rise when two or more bidders want the same lot. Good lotting puts interested bidders in the same contest.

★ Pro Tip

Why grouping can increase competition

Imagine three inexpensive, unbranded cat figurines: one red, one gray and one purple.

Red
Gray
Purple
Sarah wants the red cat.Rachel wants the gray cat.Nobody is looking for the purple one.

Sold separately: three lots

Red cat: Sarah is the only bidder$5
Gray cat: Rachel is the only bidder$5
Purple cat: no bids$0
Total$10

Each bidder wins her cat at the opening bid, because no one is bidding against her.

Grouped: one lot of three

To get the cat she wants, each must win the whole group, so Sarah and Rachel now bid against each other.

    Why it works: competitive bidding needs at least two interested bidders on the same lot. Sold separately, Sarah and Rachel never meet. Grouped, two buyers who wanted different things are in one contest, and the lot that nobody wanted rides along.

    This is an illustration, not a prediction. Real results depend on demand, condition, timing and how many bidders take part.

    Group when

    • Each item is modest on its own and would likely draw one bidder or none.
    • The pieces appeal to the same kind of buyer (kitchen glass, hand tools, craft supplies).
    • The whole group shows clearly in one photo.

    Sell individually when

    • A piece has a recognized maker, signature or brand, or meaningful collectible value.
    • It is precious metal, jewelry, fine art or a quality watch.
    • It has strong independent demand: buyers search for it by name.
    • It is worth far more than the rest; in a box lot it would be hidden from the buyers who want it.

    Quick lotting habits

    • Keep sets complete: six matching chairs or a dinner service usually sell better together.
    • One obvious buyer per lot: do not mix fishing reels with baby clothes.
    • Count and say it: "Box Lot of 24 Cookbooks" sets expectations and is searchable.
    • Spread your strongest lots through the catalog so bidders keep watching to the end.

    Lesson 3

    Photography that sells

    The first photo is what buyers see in search and on the marketplace. Make it about the merchandise.

    The primary photo

    • The whole item or group, straight on, filling most of the frame.
    • A clean, plain background and no clutter.
    • Soft, even light; no harsh flash.
    • No prominent lot label. The lot number is already on the listing.

    Supporting photos

    • Back, sides, top, underside and inside drawers.
    • Maker's marks, signatures, hallmarks, labels and model numbers.
    • Every flaw: chips, cracks, stains, repairs, missing parts.
    • A photo with the lot label, when it helps match the item at pickup.
    • A coin or tape measure for scale, when size is not obvious.

    Light, background and camera

    • Light: window daylight or overcast outdoor light. Turn off yellow overhead lights. No flash on glass, silver or framed art.
    • Background: a plain wall, sheet or poster board; lighter for most items, darker for shiny pieces.
    • Steady and level: brace your elbows, tap to focus, wipe the lens, no digital zoom. Step back instead of shooting wide up close.
    • Same order every time: primary, sides, back, marks, flaws. Cataloging from the photos then goes quickly.
    Pro Tip

    Photograph a whole table in lot order, then catalog from the photos. Smart Catalog Tools can suggest a title, category and description from your photos for you to review and edit.

    Lesson 4

    Create clear, searchable listings

    Buyers find lots by searching. A clear title brings the right bidders; an honest description keeps them happy after they win. You do not need to research prices: describe the item accurately and let the bidding set the price.

    A title formula that works

    What it is + maker or brand + material or style + one key detail (size, quantity or model).

    Instead ofNice old lamp
    Box of stuff
    RARE vintage chair!!!
    WriteArt Deco Chrome Table Lamp, Frosted Glass Shade, 18 in
    Box Lot of 24 Cookbooks, Southern and Baking
    Pair of Mid-Century Walnut Armchairs, Tweed Seats

    Every description covers

    • What it is and what is included or missing (a lid, a key, a shelf).
    • Marks exactly as they appear: maker's marks, signatures, hallmarks, labels. If unsure, write "marked" or "appears to be" rather than claiming an attribution.
    • Condition, specifically: "two chips on the rim, small stain on the left cushion", not just "good condition".
    • Dimensions and quantity: height, width and depth for furniture and art; a count for groups.
    • Pickup notes for heavy, large, upstairs or fragile items (Lesson 6).
    Pro Tip

    Avoid "rare", "antique" or "valuable" unless you can support it. "As is" tells buyers there is no warranty; it does not replace telling them what you know.

    Lesson 5

    Launch and promote your auction

    Bidders cannot compete for lots they never see.

    Featured lots

    • Pick lots with broad appeal and your best primary photos.
    • Show range: furniture, a decorative piece, a practical item.
    • The auction setup page shows how many featured lots your auction uses.

    Spread the word

    • Share the auction link with your email list and social media when the catalog opens and again before closing.
    • Professional Sellers: show it on your website widget and storefront.
    • Publish pickup details with the catalog, and allow enough preview time.
    Auction title: instead ofOnline Auction
    WriteMid-Century Furniture, Tools and Vinyl Records: Westside Estate

    Lesson 6

    Prepare for pickup day

    A calm, organized pickup earns repeat bidders. A safe one protects you and your staff.

    Do not lift, carry, disassemble or load heavy purchases for buyers.

    Under the Advantage.Bid Terms of Service, buyers are responsible for removal: they must bring enough people, equipment, packing materials and a suitable vehicle. Sellers and their staff are not required to help, and helping is how people get hurt and property gets damaged. Show buyers the item, keep the path clear, and let them do the moving.

    Say it in the listing, before bidding

    Add a pickup note to every heavy, large, upstairs or fragile lot. The same notes are in the lot editor under the description box ("Add a pickup note"); edit them to fit the item.

    Examples only: no note covers every situation. Your published pickup terms apply to every lot.

    Check and mention: stairs and tight turns, elevators or loading docks (building rules, notice needed), gated or restricted access, where a truck can park, doorway widths for oversized furniture, and anything fragile.

    Before pickup day

    • Stage sold lots by lot number or by buyer, away from unsold items.
    • Send access instructions: entrance, parking, stairs, what to bring.
    • Keep any appointments within the published pickup window.
    • Clear paths and protect floors and door frames.

    At the door

    • Check the buyer's identity and paid invoice on Advantage.Bid before anything leaves.
    • Mark each lot released on the pickup list.
    • Write down anything unusual: a missing piece, damage, a lot left behind.

    Uncollected merchandise

    Follow the pickup terms published with the auction and the platform's procedures for missed pickups, and keep a short note of what happened and your messages with the buyer. You may choose to accommodate a buyer; that does not change the published terms.

    Safety

    • Work with at least one other person; keep cash and valuables out of sight; lock rooms that are not part of the sale.
    • Payment happens on Advantage.Bid. Never accept a check or transfer for an overpayment or "the balance".

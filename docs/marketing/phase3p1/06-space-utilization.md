## Two kinds of white space

**Intentional** negative space is declared in the scene plan with a reason — the dark field around a lit crystal (REF-12), a breathing
zone beside a headline, the quiet ground of a restrained factual creative. It is excluded from the void measure and reported separately,
so the Owner can see that it was chosen.

**Accidental** white space is everything else: the area an undersized cluster leaves behind, a panel that has run out of content, a gap
between a card and the merchandise. It is measured and capped per family, and exceeding the cap by more than four points is a hard fail.

## The fit procedure (how the engine uses the canvas)

1. Compute the field from the layout's reserved copy regions.
2. Plan the cluster physically (Mission 5) at scene scale.
3. **Scale the whole cluster uniformly** until it reaches the family's minimum extent — relationships and relative scale travel with it —
   capped so the primary anchor never exceeds the field height.
4. If the cluster is still too short, **add height with a tall object** (floor lamp, cabinet, tall sculpture, art hung above an anchor)
   from the eligible set. Never stretch; never enlarge a small object past its scale tolerance.
5. If coverage is still under the band, **add objects** by anchor weight then foreground suitability; re-plan; re-audit. Never inflate a
   bowl to fill a corner — that is exactly how Individual Seller A's bowl reached table height.
6. If an undeclared void exceeds the cap, **reposition** the cluster toward it (respecting copy regions) before any other change.
7. If the band still cannot be met with the CLEAN objects available, **change family** — fewer, larger objects (LEFT_THIRD_WHITE), or
   one object (SINGLE_LOT) — rather than accept an under-filled canvas.

Copy regions are protected throughout: a cluster that would enter one is re-planned, not clipped.

## Panels and cards

A panel or card (the information column of LEFT_THIRD_WHITE, a contact card) must earn its area: its content should occupy at least
55% of its height; if not, the panel shrinks to its content or the layout changes. Individual Seller C's card and West University B's
panel both fail this rule. A contact card is never the second-largest element of an acquisition creative; the merchandise is.

## How this reads in the scorer

Coverage, extent, void and upper-field share move from the soft measurable block into **hard floors and caps** with a narrow soft band
above them (config `gates`). A creative that fails a floor is regenerated by the fit procedure, not scored. The measurable block reports
the field-based numbers alongside the old canvas-based ones so calibration history stays comparable.

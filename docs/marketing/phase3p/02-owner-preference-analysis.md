# Deliverable 2 — Owner Preference Analysis

**Phase 3P · 2026-09-09 · Source: the 13 references in Deliverable 1, read collectively.**

The question this document answers is not "what do these ads look like" but "why does the Owner approve them, and which parts of that are
his taste versus Lewis & Maese's identity or the event's own character". Section A is what Advantage.Bid may adopt. Section B is what must
stay with the seller or the event. Section C records the tensions between what the Owner approved and what he has said in earlier phases —
those are the most useful findings, because they show where a rule is too rigid.

## A. Transferable Owner preferences (recurring across the library)

**A1. Identity leads, then the event, then the merchandise carries the rest.** 13/13 identify the seller before anything else competes (11 at the
top, REF-01 bottom-left as the closing element, REF-12 via the URL only). The hierarchy the Owner accepts is short: presenter → one event title → essential facts →
close. He does not approve ads that open with a slogan.

**A2. Merchandise-forward composition.** Merchandise holds an estimated 45–85% of the canvas in 12/13 references; the exception is the holiday
greeting (REF-11). In the low-copy references the merchandise:text ratio is roughly 5:1 to 7:1 (REF-02, REF-08, REF-13). The Owner's eye wants
objects big.

**A3. One dominant title word, and it is usually the event type.** AUCTION is the largest word in REF-01, REF-03, REF-04; ESTATE SALE in
REF-09/10; MANCAVE/GOLD in the themed ads. Titles are 2–5 words. Date typography is the second-largest text element (REF-04, REF-05/06, REF-09,
REF-13). Time is always smaller than date.

**A4. Restrained copy, with a ceiling that depends on the job.** Median copy count is 5 blocks. The two references the Owner filed as the best
general-auction examples (REF-02, REF-13) carry 2–3 blocks. Density rises only where the facts are the sell (category auctions with logistics,
REF-03/07) and is organised into one column when it does.

**A5. Light ground by default; colour comes from the merchandise.** 8/13 are white or cream; the objects supply the colour (turquoise vase,
amethyst, red chairs, pink geode). The single dark ground (REF-12) is a lit hero object. This is the 3M.1 decision, confirmed by the Owner's own
selections without prompting.

**A6. Grounded, believable scenes when a scene is claimed.** REF-02, REF-04, REF-05, REF-06 all obey the Owner's later-stated spatial logic:
art on the wall plane, heavy furniture on a shared floor, small objects in front, overlaps that mean "in front of". Two references (REF-07,
REF-08) claim no scene at all and float objects freely — see C3.

**A7. Editorial scale variation and confident edges.** Anchors exist in every scene reference (dining table, cabinet, painting, vase);
supporting objects are mid-scale; foreground objects are small. Objects exit the frame at the right/bottom in REF-02, REF-04, REF-06, REF-08 —
never cutting a dial, a signature or a face.

**A8. Typography that tells the truth about the event.** GOLD in gold (REF-13); distressed western display for a mancave sale (REF-07/08);
script "Estate Sale" against formal caps for a neighbourhood sale (REF-10); the numeral 725+ as the hero (REF-05/06). The Owner rewards
type that carries meaning, and he applies it to one word or one element, not to everything.

**A9. A closing band with the web address.** 7/13 end with a full-width or panel-width band carrying the URL. Colours vary by seller and event;
the device is constant. The 3M.2 navy Advantage.Bid band is exactly this device.

**A10. Quiet, factual CTAs or none.** "Bid online at <url>" (online-only auction), "View the video preview" (a real asset), "Bid with
confidence" (reassurance), or nothing. No urgency language appears anywhere in the library.

**A11. Category breadth is welcome.** Vehicles, a bulldozer, a boat, a golf cart, arcade cabinet, taxidermy, tools, memorabilia sit beside
bronzes and porcelain across the library. The Owner does not select an antiques-only picture of the business.

**A12. Variation inside a campaign.** He approved both halves of two campaign pairs (REF-01/12, REF-05/06). The brand frame stays, the
merchandise and composition change. This is "standardize the brand frame, customize the event experience" as a revealed preference.

**A13. Format fluency.** Square, 3:2 landscape and tall portrait are all present; the same hierarchy survives each. Tall portrait is used
for the on-site sale poster (REF-09).

**A14. Environment for on-site sales, extraction for auctions.** Both estate-sale references show the actual house. Every auction reference
uses extracted objects, a staged still life, tiles or a lineup. This distinction is currently absent from the Advantage.Bid family list.

## C. Tensions between approvals and earlier stated rules (where rules need scoping)

**C1. "No object lineups" vs REF-13.** The 3M rule ("clusters with depth rather than object lineups") was written for broad scene ads. The
Owner approves a lineup for a homogeneous category (watches). Resolution: scope the rule to the scene families; allow LINEUP for
category groups where comparison is the point.

**C2. "Less text / 5 blocks" vs REF-03 and REF-07 (8 blocks).** Both are category or themed auctions with real logistics (in-house + online,
address, preview days). Resolution: the 5-block budget stays for general families; introduce an explicit LOGISTICS budget (up to 7 blocks,
icon-led, single column) that a brief must opt into with a factual reason. REF-11's 7 blocks are a holiday card and do not count as evidence.

**C3. Gravity rule vs REF-07/REF-08 (floating objects).** The gravity/wall/surface rules were locked for the centered scene family after
3M.2 Version B. The mancave references claim no room; objects float as a catalogue spread. Resolution: keep gravity locked where a scene is
claimed; add a rationed CATALOG_SCATTER family with its own rules (no gravity claim, uniform light shadow, protected regions still enforced,
never the default, limited to teaser waves and themed/eclectic auctions). This also directly addresses the "not every ad a collage" boundary.

**C4. "No CTA lines" vs REF-01/12/03/07.** The Owner does not select hype, but he does accept one quiet factual CTA. Resolution: allow one
factual CTA slot (online-only → "Bid online at advantage.bid"; a real preview → "Preview available") counted inside the budget; still no
"Don't miss out" style copy.

**C5. Title position.** 3M.2/3M.3 place the centered title at the top. REF-02 puts it at the bottom under the merchandise. Resolution: an
approved variation of CENTERED_WHITE (title-bottom) when merchandise rises to the presenter line; not a new default.

**C6. Merchandise share.** The certified B3 composition is 41.6% merchandise / 22.3% text region. The Owner's references in the same
families are estimated at 55–72% merchandise / 8–18% text. This is consistent with his repeated "make it bigger" and the CALIBRATION EXTREME
request. Resolution: raise the scene-family calibration band for merchandise to roughly 50–65% and cap the text region near 18%, to be
confirmed with the first proving ground, not locked here.

**C7. Dark ground.** The 3M.1 decision (dark navy is a specialised treatment, not an equal default) is confirmed, and the trigger is now
specific: one lit hero object.

**C8. Category line.** The Owner said "no piles of category labels"; he approved one short category line in REF-05/06/09. Resolution: the
existing "one short supporting line" slot may be a category line of six words or fewer.

## D. What the library cannot tell us (and must not be guessed)

- Nothing about the Professional Seller + Advantage.Bid co-brand line — no reference shows both marks. The relationship line remains
  uncalibrated until the West University proving ground is reviewed.
- Nothing about acquisition creative (individual or professional seller), buyer growth, or closing-soon creative. Those classes are empty and
  the engine must treat them as "global principles only, Owner review mandatory".
- Nothing about performance. The Owner selected on taste; no metric was attached to any file. Approval and performance remain separate signals.
- Whether the Owner prefers square, landscape or portrait — all three are present and none was singled out.

## E. The Owner preference statement (one paragraph, for the brief)

An Owner-approved Advantage.Bid advertisement leads with the presenter, states the event in a few large words, lets real merchandise fill
most of a light canvas with the colour it brings, arranges objects so that heavy things sit, art hangs and small things come forward, uses
type that tells the truth about the event and stops when the essential facts are on the page, and closes with the navy Advantage.Bid band.
It varies from creative to creative within a campaign and never looks like a template with the merchandise swapped.
